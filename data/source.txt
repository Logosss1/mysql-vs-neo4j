Summary results for virtual practical lab 1
1. Users. What types of users students mentioned in their submission?

Elders, seniors, and caregivers, older Americans (people over the age of 65, for example), close
family members of the elderly; older adults ages 55 and above, older adults and their support
groups, library staff, community organizations; members of the elderly community (> 65 years
old); social workers, and healthcare providers.
Question: who is consider an elder?

           •   55 or older
           •   60 or older,
           •   65 or older
2. Topic or focus of the collection

Identification of elder fraud and pathways for legal recourse
Internet safety focuses on identifying AI advertisements, videos, and voices; identifying
phishing emails; and other general internet safety rules.
Phone scams: to identify spam calls, a third section would focus on credit card fraud and mail
fraud, and a fourth section would describe different financial services (specifically home
refinancing services).
Tactics employed to carry out the scam, i.e., financial services scams, romance or
“grandchild” scams, tech support scams, government impersonation scams, phone scams, and
Medicare or health insurance scams, and how in almost all cases the scammer.
Elder fraud at my library.

Book clubs, author talks, and exercise classes keep people active and engaging with others.
Information from the Department of Aging, Senior Centers in the county, and Maryland Legal
Aid.
3. Outcomes or goals of the collection
Building collections for the elderly and their caretakers

Creating an infographic, checklist, or interactive prompt designed to help users identify
warning signs of elder fraud. Examples could include unexpected requests for money or personal
information, suspicious phone calls or emails, pressure to act immediately, or requests to use
unusual payment methods.
Videos to demonstrate common types of elder fraud, explain preventative measures, and provide
examples of what someone can do if they suspect they have been targeted. Videos could also

Having stories that showcase the experiences to remove that stigma.
Creating a digital portal with videos covering similar subjects made specifically for the
collection.
Organize regular digital seminars for elderly people to learn best with a more conversational
setting, and it feels more real to them to hear a person speaking to and addressing them.

In-person classes and presentations in libraries, VFWs, and even churches would be the
most beneficial for the elders themselves.
For tax season, have state and federal forms printed and available for people to take at public
libraries.
Public program partnership with AARP Tax Help to help elderly people who need help filing
their taxes.
Seeking knowledge and information on elder fraud and its prevention and mitigation.
